# Results — Convex Volatility Interpolation

Reference paper: Deschâtres, F. *Convex Volatility Interpolation*.

This file is regenerated by hand after each run. Numbers come directly from the
CSV files in this directory; verdicts follow the thresholds in the repository's
operating notes (reproduced when within 10% and same sign, partially reproduced
otherwise, not reproduced when the sign differs or the gap exceeds 50% and no
paper range covers it).

## Experiment 1 — CVI calibration as a quadratic program with iteratively linearized butterfly-arbitrage constraints

The script `exp/exp1_calibrate.py` builds the full CVI pipeline described in
Sections "Analysis of the Problem" through "Putting It All Together"
(pp. 1–6): variance-space parameterization as a cubic spline over normalized
log-moneyness, the four-term objective (least-squares fit with inverse
squared-spread weights, above-ask and below-bid hinge-squared penalties with
vega-weighted ask weights, and total variation of knot convexities with weight
λ = 0.05), and the complete set of positivity, calendar, Lee-tail and interior
butterfly constraints. The butterfly constraints are linearized around the
previous iterate (eq. (2) and surrounding text, p. 5) and the problem is solved
as a quadratic program with Clarabel through CVXPY. One initial pass without
the linearized butterfly block is followed by one refinement pass with all
constraints, matching the two-iteration setup of the paper's numerical section
(p. 6).

Fitted artifacts for each run are saved as:

- `exp1_summary.csv` — per-run totals (variables, constraints, nnz(P), nnz(A),
  solver time, average χ², constraint-violation minima).
- `exp1_per_expiry_<asset>.csv` — per-expiry tenor, forward, anchor σ*, quote
  counts, and χ².
- `exp1_arbitrage_<asset>.csv` — minimum slack on positivity, calendar, Lee and
  butterfly constraints evaluated on a dense verification grid.
- `exp1_smile_<asset>.png` — fitted smiles with bid/ask quotes for a selection
  of expiries.

Minimum slacks on the dense verification grid are reported in
`exp1_arbitrage_<asset>.csv`. On the constraint grid used by the QP
(`N_DISCRETIZATION = 60`, denser than the 20 of the paper's numerical
study on p. 6), all four blocks hold to Clarabel's default tolerance.
On the verification grid the slacks are: positivity ≥ 0 on both assets;
calendar violation ≈ −1 × 10⁻³ (equal to the Lee strict-inequality margin
used in the QP); Lee tail slopes strict (SPY +4.9 × 10⁻², QQQ +2.4 × 10⁻¹);
interior butterfly +5.8 × 10⁻³ on SPY and −9.8 × 10⁻² on QQQ. The QQQ
butterfly minimum is a residual nonlinearity error of the linearization in
the shortest-tenor deep wings between constraint knots; it is small compared
to typical convexity magnitudes (|c| of order 1) but strictly negative, which
is why the butterfly row above is marked "partially reproduced".

## Experiment 2 — Scaling of CVI calibration with Clarabel

The script `exp/exp2_scaling.py` runs the same CVI pipeline across a range of
knot counts per expiry and records, for every setting: number of variables,
number of constraints in the second iteration, nnz(P), nnz(A), setup time and
Clarabel solver time summed over both iterations, and the average χ² across
expiries. The paper reports these quantities for SPX and SX5E in Tables 2–3 and
discusses the chi-squared plateau and the sparsity-growth laws in the "Scaling"
section (p. 7).

## Deviations from the paper

- **Underlying names.** The paper's numerical study uses index options on SPX,
  SX5E, NDX and KOSPI2 and single-name options on AMZN, MSFT, NVDA (p. 6).
  Those datasets are entitled vendor feeds. This replication uses listed U.S.
  equity-option snapshot quotes on the ETFs SPY and QQQ, which track the S&P
  500 and the Nasdaq-100 referenced by the paper. The CVI formulation, the
  objective, the constraint set and the two-pass QP schedule are unchanged;
  only the underlying list differs.
- **Snapshot source for bid and ask IVs.** The paper assumes consolidated
  NBBO bid/ask implied volatilities per contract. The snapshot used here
  reports mid implied volatilities only. Synthetic bid/ask half-widths are
  built from a two-piece function of moneyness (ATM half-width plus a wing
  slope term in |z|). The CVI pipeline — in particular the objective's
  inverse-spread weights w = 1/(v_ask - v_bid)² and the vega-weighted
  above-ask / below-bid penalties — is unchanged; only the input bid/ask
  numbers come from the synthetic model. The two constants are listed in
  `src/config.py` as `SPREAD_HALF_WIDTH_ATM` and `SPREAD_HALF_WIDTH_WING_SLOPE`.
- **Snapshot time.** The paper does not state the snapshot time of its
  datasets (p. 6). Here it is whichever snapshot the exchange endpoint returns
  at run time.
- **De-Americanization.** The paper mentions de-Americanizing single-name
  implied volatilities (p. 6) but does not specify the procedure. This
  replication uses ETF options with exchange-reported implied volatilities and
  does not apply an additional de-Americanization step.
- **ATM anchor σ\*(T).** The paper does not specify how σ\*(T) is estimated
  before the fit. This replication reads it as the quoted implied volatility
  of the option with strike closest to the forward in each expiry.
- **Implied-volatility outlier filter.** Stale listed quotes occasionally
  produce point-wise implied volatilities well off the smile. A median filter
  over `IV_OUTLIER_WINDOW_K` strikes per expiry with half-width
  `IV_OUTLIER_HALF_WIDTH` removes those outliers before calibration. The
  paper does not describe such a filter. Both constants are in `src/config.py`.
- **Positivity constraint on every expiry.** The paper imposes variance
  positivity on the discretization grid for the first expiry only and relies
  on the calendar constraints to propagate positivity to subsequent expiries
  ("Non-Arbitrage-Related Constraints", p. 3). With the 20-point grid used in
  the paper's numerical study (p. 6) this implication can fail numerically on
  some ETF surfaces. The implementation imposes positivity on every expiry on
  the same grid; the additional block has `J · n_disc` inequalities and does
  not change the objective.
- **Iterative linearization halt.** The paper describes one or a few
  refinement passes (p. 6). When the linearized butterfly block makes a
  refinement pass numerically infeasible, the implementation keeps the
  previous iterate as the final one and records the halt in the `status`
  column. In addition, grid points where the current iterate's variance is at
  or below the positivity floor (dominated anyway by the positivity block)
  are skipped from the butterfly linearization to keep its gradients well
  conditioned.
- **Sparsity of the P matrix.** The paper reports nnz(P) that scales
  quadratically with the number of parameters per expiry (p. 7). The
  replication measures nnz(P) and nnz(A) on the matrices Clarabel actually
  receives after CVXPY canonicalization. CVXPY introduces auxiliary variables
  for the sum-of-squares terms and moves the quadratic cost into epigraph
  equalities, which keeps nnz(P) constant across knot counts while nnz(A)
  absorbs the quadratic cost (hence the linear growth of nnz(A) is
  reproduced but the quadratic growth of nnz(P) is not).

## Not reproduced

- The exact wall-clock timings reported in Tables 2–3 of the paper, because
  the paper's hardware and index-option datasets are not available in this
  environment. The scaling trends (χ² plateau, approximately linear nnz(A)
  growth, approximately linear runtime growth) are reproduced on SPY and QQQ.
- Figure 6 for SPX, SX5E, NDX, KOSPI2, AMZN, MSFT and NVDA (p. 7): the
  underlying datasets are not available in this environment. The χ²-vs-knots
  curve and the sparsity curves are produced on SPY and QQQ in
  `exp2_fig6.csv` / `exp2_chi2_vs_knots.png` and
  `exp2_sparsity_vs_knots.png`.
